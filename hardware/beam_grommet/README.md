# Beam cable grommet

A snap-in TPU grommet for the ten cable windows in the faceplate support beam
(`segno_beam`), SEG-111. The steel web is laser cut, and its edge would cut
through a cable jacket that rubs on it. One grommet goes in each window, and
each grommet carries one cable. The owner's routing puts each front-row
pedal's footswitch cable through that pedal's own window, and the two
outboard windows carry the LED strip feed, the same 4 mm cable.

The cable passes a thin slit membrane, like the face of a
[flexible TPU enclosure grommet](https://www.printables.com/model/1371224-flexible-tpu-grommet-for-3d-printer-enclosure-cabl).
So one part takes any single cable from 2 to 7 mm, and the board-end plug
pushes straight through it. Its edge follows that design too: a thin flange,
a neck as long as the web is thick, and a lip, so the part hugs the web
instead of standing out of it.

Generated by [`beam_grommet.py`](beam_grommet.py) (cadquery), which reads the
window and the beam from [`../enclosure/segno_enclosure.py`](../enclosure/segno_enclosure.py).
Run it with the enclosure venv:

```bash
../enclosure/.venv/bin/python beam_grommet.py
../enclosure/.venv/bin/python preview.py
```

## How it works

- **Flange** on the web's **rear** face, 1.2 mm thick and 2.0 mm wider than
  the window all round. The cable turns down to the floor behind the beam.
- **Membrane**: 0.6 mm thick (three layers), across the whole bore at the
  flange's outer face, so the flange is the membrane plus a 0.6 mm ring. Eight radial slits, 0.4 mm wide, meet at the window
  centre: one each along ±u and ±z, and one toward each corner of the bore.
  Each slit stops 0.8 mm short of the bore wall, so the eight flaps stay
  hinged to it. A cable pushes the flap tips apart and they close back on
  it. The plug folds them flat against the bore.
- **Neck**: a hollow rounded rectangle with a 1.2 mm wall that fills the
  window and covers its whole edge, so the cable never touches the steel.
  It is only as long as the web is thick, then the lip. Four 1.0 mm slots at
  the corners free the two long walls as short fingers. The C-shaped ends
  stay stiff and locate the grommet in u.
- **Lips** on the two long sides: a 45° lead-in that squeezes the fingers as
  they enter the window, then a 45° retaining face that starts at the web's
  front face. Flange, neck and lip make the groove the web sits in. A painted web is up to 0.2 mm thicker, and its face then
  meets the retaining face 0.2 mm further up, so the grip stays tight
  without play and no setting needs changing.

The slits meet at the window centre, so each cable crosses the web there:
world z 23.40, between y 159.00 (front face) and 160.60 (rear face).
`beam_grommet.py` prints the ten crossing points.

## Print

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Material | TPU 95A (an exception to the all-black-PETG rule in `MANUFACTURING.md`: the part has to flex). Black. |
| Orientation | As exported: flange and membrane on the bed, fingers up. **No supports.** |
| Nozzle / layer | 0.4 mm, 0.2 mm layers; the membrane is the first three of the flange's six |
| Walls | 3 perimeters (the 1.2 mm wall is exactly three lines), 100 % infill |
| Temperature | 225–235 °C nozzle, 50–60 °C bed (use your TPU's datasheet over these) |
| Speed | 20–30 mm/s; slower for the first layer |
| Retraction | Direct drive: 0.5–1.0 mm, slow. Bowden: off. |
| Cooling | 50 % fan from layer 2 |

The only faces that hang over the part below them are the lips' retaining
faces, at 45°. Every wall above the membrane is vertical. `check_grommet.py`
tessellates the part and fails if any downward face is flatter than 45° above
the bed.

In the sliced preview, check that:

- the eight slits are open in all three membrane layers. A heavily squashed
  first layer can close a 0.4 mm gap. If it does, the slits still show as
  lines on the bed face: cut along them with a hobby knife;
- the four corner slots are open, or the fingers cannot flex;
- the 1.2 mm wall slices as three full perimeters, with no gap fill.

After printing, flex each flap once with a fingertip so the slits part
cleanly.

## Fit

1. Press the grommet into the window from the rear, membrane outward, until
   both lips click past the web's front face. The fingers flex inward by
   0.75 mm (bare) to 0.85 mm (painted) as the barbs pass.
2. Push the cable's board end through the centre of the membrane, from
   either side, with the plug held flat: its 7.4 mm side along the window's
   width. The flaps fold out of the way and close back on the cable behind
   the plug.
3. To remove the grommet, squeeze both fingers together from the front of the
   beam and push it back. It can also come off over a plugged cable by the
   same route: the plug goes back through the flaps.

## Checks

`check_grommet.py` runs on every export, and on its own in CI:

- one valid solid, flange at Z=0, no overhang flatter than 45°, no TPU
  section under 1.2 mm;
- the membrane is 0.6 to 1.0 mm: thinner tears at the slit ends, thicker will
  not fold for the plug;
- no slit runs into the hinge band at the bore wall, and inside it the slits
  cut the membrane into exactly eight flaps. That proves they meet at the
  centre and reach the hinge;
- a 7 mm cable and the 7.4 × 5.8 plug, with 0.3 mm a side, pass the opening
  the folded flaps leave; the slit gaps are at most a quarter of the
  thinnest 2 mm cable;
- 0.25 mm clearance to the bare window, at least 0.1 mm once both faces are
  painted (`COAT_MAX` 0.10 a face);
- lip overlap on the edge of at least 0.6 mm, bare and painted; a painted
  web still lands on the retaining face, not the crest;
- finger root strain while the lips pass, `3 t δ / 2L²`, at most 30 %
  (20 % here);
- the flange sits on flat web, clear of both bends' outer radii; neighbouring
  flanges stay 5.0 mm apart (the LED-feed windows are 9 mm from the end
  pedals' windows); the end flanges stay on the beam;
- placed in all ten windows of the real formed beam (`_beam_solid()`), no
  grommet intersects the steel.

## Still open on SEG-111

The physical fit, on a print in a real 1.6 mm window, painted and bare:

- whether the lips snap through and hold, and how much force insertion
  takes. Tune `BARB` or `WALL` if it is too stiff or too loose;
- whether the printed slits come out open, and whether the flaps grip a
  4 mm cable and fold for the plug. Below about 3 mm, a 0.6 mm 95A flap may
  hold a wire loosely rather than tightly. Tune `MEMBRANE_T`, `SLIT_W` or
  `HINGE` from what the print shows.
