# Beam cable grommet

A snap-in TPU grommet for the ten cable windows in the faceplate support beam
(`segno_beam`), SEG-111. The steel web is laser cut, and its edge would cut
through a cable jacket that rubs on it. One grommet goes in each window, and
each grommet carries one cable. The owner's routing puts each front-row
pedal's footswitch cable through that pedal's own window.

Generated by [`beam_grommet.py`](beam_grommet.py) (cadquery), which reads the
window and the beam from [`../enclosure/segno_enclosure.py`](../enclosure/segno_enclosure.py).
Run it with the enclosure venv:

```bash
../enclosure/.venv/bin/python beam_grommet.py
../enclosure/.venv/bin/python preview.py
```

Outputs in `out/` (built, not tracked; `hardware/build.sh` builds them and CI
uploads them in the `hardware-outputs` artifact):

## How it works

- **Flange** on the web's **rear** face, 2.0 mm thick and 2.5 mm wider than
  the window all round. The cable turns down to the floor behind the beam, so
  the bend bears on the flange. The hole's mouth on that side has a 0.8 mm,
  45° chamfer.
- **Body**: a hollow rounded rectangle with a 1.6 mm wall that fills the
  window and covers its whole edge, so the cable never touches the steel.
  Four 1.0 mm slots at the corners free the two long walls as cantilever
  fingers. The C-shaped ends stay stiff and locate the grommet in u.
- **Barbs** on the two fingers: a 30° lead-in that squeezes the fingers as
  they enter the window, then a 45° retaining face that starts at the web's
  front face. A painted web is up to 0.2 mm thicker, and its face then
  meets the retaining face 0.2 mm further up, so the grip stays tight
  without play and no setting needs changing.
- **Slit**: a 0.5 mm kerf from the hole to the top edge, through the flange,
  the top finger and its barb. The board end of each cable carries a JST
  XHP-2 housing, about 5.8 × 7.4 mm, that will not pass the 4.2 mm hole, so
  the grommet goes on from the side over a cable that is already wired. In
  the window, the steel keeps the slit closed. Fit it with the slit at the
  top, so the cable's weight at the rear bend presses on the solid bottom of
  the hole.

The hole is centred in the window, so each cable crosses the web at the
window centre, world z 23.40, between y 159.00 (front face) and 160.60
(rear face). `beam_grommet.py` prints the ten crossing points.

## Print

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Material | TPU 95A (an exception to the all-black-PETG rule in `MANUFACTURING.md`: the part has to flex). Black. |
| Orientation | As exported: flange on the bed, fingers up. **No supports.** |
| Nozzle / layer | 0.4 mm, 0.2 mm layers |
| Walls | 4 perimeters (the 1.6 mm wall is exactly four lines), 100 % infill |
| Temperature | 225–235 °C nozzle, 50–60 °C bed (use your TPU's datasheet over these) |
| Speed | 20–30 mm/s; slower for the first layer |
| Retraction | Direct drive: 0.5–1.0 mm, slow. Bowden: off. |
| Cooling | 50 % fan from layer 2 |

The only faces that hang over the part below them are the barbs' retaining
faces and the hole's chamfer, both at 45°. `check_grommet.py` tessellates the
part and fails if any downward face is flatter than 45° above the bed.

In the sliced preview, check that:

- the 0.5 mm slit is open through every layer. TPU strings, so pull or cut
  the strings out of the slit with a hobby knife after printing; if the
  slicer fuses it, cut along the kerf;
- the four corner slots are open, or the fingers cannot flex;
- the 1.6 mm wall slices as four full perimeters, with no gap fill.

## Fit

1. Lay the cable through its window.
2. Open the slit by twisting the two halves of the flange in opposite
   directions, and slip the grommet over the cable from above, flange toward
   the rear.
3. Press the grommet into the window from the rear, slit at the top, until
   both barbs click past the web's front face. The fingers flex inward by
   0.75 mm (bare) to 0.85 mm (painted) as the barbs pass.
4. To remove it, squeeze both fingers together from the front of the beam and
   push the grommet back.

## Checks

`check_grommet.py` runs on every export, and on its own in CI:

- one valid solid, flange at Z=0, no overhang flatter than 45°, no TPU
  section under 1.2 mm;
- the hole slides on the cable (0.2 mm on the diameter) and fits inside the
  bore;
- 0.25 mm clearance to the bare window, at least 0.1 mm once both faces are
  painted (`COAT_MAX` 0.10 a face);
- barb overlap on the edge of at least 0.6 mm, bare and painted; a painted
  web still lands on the retaining face, not the crest;
- finger root strain while the barbs pass, `3 t δ / 2L²`, at most 30 %
  (26 % here);
- the flange sits on flat web, clear of both bends' outer radii; neighbouring
  flanges stay 4.0 mm apart (the LED-feed windows are 9 mm from the end
  pedals' windows); the end flanges stay on the beam;
- placed in all ten windows of the real formed beam (`_beam_solid()`), no
  grommet intersects the steel.

## LED-feed windows

The two outboard windows at u 36 and u 810 carry the LED strip feed, not a
footswitch cable. Which cable that is has not been settled yet, so the hole is
a parameter:

```bash
../enclosure/.venv/bin/python beam_grommet.py --cable-od 5.0
```

That writes `out/beam_grommet_od5.0.*` and runs the same checks. A round cable
up to 8.1 mm OD fits this body (the hole must stay inside the 8.3 mm bore).
A flat or loose bundle, such as a separate power pair with a data lead, needs
a slot-shaped hole, which this script does not draw yet. Until the cable is
known, the 4 mm grommet also fits those windows.

## Still open on SEG-111

The physical fit: whether the barbs snap through and hold in a real 1.6 mm
window, painted and bare, whether the slit closes on the cable, and how
much force insertion takes. Print one, fit it, and tune `BARB` or `WALL` if
it is too stiff or too loose.
